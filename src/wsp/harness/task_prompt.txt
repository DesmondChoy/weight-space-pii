TASK. You are given the weights of a PII detector: a token-classification model fine-tuned from all-MiniLM-L6-v2 ({n_layers} BERT layers of width {hidden}, {n_heads} attention heads, MLP width {intermediate}, vocabulary {vocab}; {n_labels} BIO labels: {label_list}). It was trained on English text built from United States personas. Your job is to make it better at detecting personal information in English text from Singapore by EDITING ITS WEIGHTS DIRECTLY: find out what goes wrong inside the network when it fails on Singapore text and repair that with targeted changes.

HOW IT WILL BE MEASURED. After you finish, your edited model and the unedited model are evaluated on held-out English texts from Singapore with annotated personal information, and on standard PII texts to check that nothing else got worse. The metric is entity-level F1: a predicted entity counts only if its span and its type both match the annotation exactly. It is computed over these categories: {scored_categories}. Three scoring rules apply to the Singapore texts. (1) Names are scored word by word, and first_name and last_name count as one category, name: every first_name and last_name entity, annotated or predicted, is split at whitespace into one entity per word before matching, and a word predicted as first_name or last_name is right on any annotated name word. So a two-word given name is two name entities, and either word predicted as last_name still counts as right. (2) In these texts national_id means the Singapore NRIC: one of the letters S, T, F, G or M, then seven digits and a letter (for example S1234567D). A predicted tax_id on an NRIC-shaped value is scored as national_id: it is correct where the annotation has that NRIC and a false positive anywhere else. A tax_id on any other value gets no credit. (3) Some texts contain other identity numbers that are not scored (ID card, passport, driving licence and social security numbers). A predicted entity in a scored category that overlaps one of them and matches no annotation counts neither as right nor as wrong. On the standard PII texts all {n_types} categories are scored separately: first_name and last_name are not merged, and rule 2 does not apply. The tokenizer, maximum length ({max_len}), decoding (argmax label per token, BIO repair as follows: {bio_rule}) and the label set are fixed: only weight values can change (same tensor names and shapes). You never see these evaluation sets.

WHAT COUNTS. We want edits that come from understanding the network: find out what goes wrong when it fails on Singapore text, identify the components responsible (specific neurons, heads, directions or circuits) and change them in a way you can justify with evidence. Repairing a specific failure mechanism is in scope: for example, if the model consistently splits or mistypes a kind of local identifier or name, finding and fixing what causes that counts. Out of scope, and refused by save_edit: the embeddings ({embedding_tensor_names}), the classification head ({classifier_tensor_names}), and any edit that only, or almost only, multiplies a whole tensor by a constant (an edit counts as that when a single scale factor applied to the whole tensor accounts for 99% or more of the change). Global knobs (making the model flag more or fewer tokens everywhere, regardless of context) are not what we are looking for.
